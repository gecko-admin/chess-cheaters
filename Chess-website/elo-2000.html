Set the strongest computer a precise problem

Play 2000 Elo chess when you want the longest search available from the Chess Cheaters computer. The setting lets the engine examine moves for longer than the lower levels, with the depth reached depending on the position and device. The number is an estimate of difficulty. An individual result does not establish a tournament rating. For a revealing test, bring a position where you liked your prospects but could never demonstrate a clear route forward. Decide what would count as progress before the game starts.

Choose a side and write down your assessment in terms you can test. “White can invade on c7 once the queens come off” is more useful than “White is better”. Now name Black’s strongest source of counterplay. It might be a checking sequence that makes a queen exchange difficult, a pawn break that closes the c-file or a sacrifice that removes the invading rook. Starting with competing claims gives even a short game a question worth answering.

Calculate a branching defence

In a tactical position, begin with the reply you expect, then deliberately replace it. If a rook gives check on the back rank, can the defending king move instead of taking the rook? If a bishop appears trapped, can its owner insert a check before retreating it? List the legal ways to answer the forcing move and discard them for concrete reasons. An unexpected king step can alter the geometry of a combination without changing the material count at all.

For a second pass, reverse the order of your intended moves. An immediate capture may allow an intermediate check, whereas giving your own check first could remove that resource. Equally, the preliminary check might drive the king onto the very square that protects your target. Compare the two resulting positions piece by piece. The worthwhile discovery is the single defender, tempo or flight square that makes the move order matter.

Convert an advantage against active resistance

Try an ending in which you have an extra queenside pawn and each side still has a rook. Mark the file where your passer would like to advance. Before pushing, ask whether your rook can remain active and whether your king has shelter from side checks. A pawn on the seventh rank can be difficult to advance if its own king has nowhere to hide and the rook is condemned to guard it. Stop at the moment the pawn push becomes irreversible and calculate the opponent’s most active reply.

Play that ending again as the defender. Seek checks from a distance, threats against the enemy pawns or a chance to give up your rook for the last dangerous passer under favourable circumstances. Study the difference between defending a weakness and forcing the attacking side to spend time. If the attacker repeatedly has to stop and shield the king, your rook may already have found a useful defensive job.

Challenge the engine’s suggestion

Use Find best move at a position where your own calculation disagreed with the computer’s choice. First predict the proposed move’s purpose without playing it. Does it close a file, remove a checking square or simply keep a second threat available? Then try to disprove it on the board. A search result is a candidate backed by a finite calculation. Particularly in quiet endings, you can learn as much from understanding the engine’s horizon as from reproducing its first move.

Keep one comparison fair by restoring the same initial position and changing only the candidate under examination. If your alternative wins a pawn, allow the computer its best counterplay before judging the outcome. Note the first moment the resulting position becomes easier or harder to handle. A material gain that demands a succession of only moves can be a poor practical choice even if you find a path through it afterwards.

Keep the starting position for another day

Save a resume link at the position you want to revisit and label it with the decision, such as “rook invasion or queenside majority”. Reopen it after the original calculation has faded and choose a move before consulting the engine again. Compare the depth of your explanation as well as your answer. Spotting a previously missed defence is progress even when you ultimately select the same move. Over several sessions, these positions become a record of the kinds of problems that actually occur in your games. Revisit one after a week and see which defensive idea you remember first.
