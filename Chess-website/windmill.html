Set up The Windmill

In Moscow in 1925, Carlos Torre reached this position against Emanuel Lasker. White to move, with the queens facing each other across the fifth rank. There is one obstacle between them. White's own bishop stands on g5. Moving it would expose both queens to attack at once. You might reasonably decide to leave the bishop where it is until you have worked out whose queen would be taken first.

There is another relationship to notice before you play. White has a rook on g3 and Black has a pawn on g7 in front of the king. Right now the bishop on g5 also interrupts that rook's path. Moving one piece could set two other pieces free at the same time. Look for a new home for the bishop from which it could join the attack after stepping aside. Give the board a try before reading the next section.

A bishop aimed at an empty square

Trace the short diagonal from f6 to h8. A bishop on f6 would watch the corner through g7 whenever g7 stood empty. A king on h8 would then be in check. With a white rook placed on g7 instead, the same bishop would protect the rook, even though its view of h8 was temporarily blocked. These two states of the g7-square are the heart of the windmill tactic.

Torre begins by moving the bishop to f6. Lasker's queen travels from b5 along the newly opened rank and takes White's queen on h5. Now the white rook can sweep up from g3 to take the g7-pawn with check. Consider the black king's choices. It cannot capture the rook because of the bishop, and its own knight occupies f8. It moves into the corner, ready for a completely different kind of check.

Listen for the change of checking piece

With the king on h8, the rook takes the pawn on f7. A rook on f7 attacks neither h8 nor the diagonal leading to it. Lift your finger off g7 and you can see the actual check arriving from the bishop on f6. Black's king goes back to g8. White puts the rook back on g7 with a direct check, and the king returns to h8. Running through that little circuit slowly makes the mechanics easier to remember.

The next trip is more ambitious. From g7 the rook travels all the way to b7 and captures a bishop. Trace the route along the seventh rank. The f7-pawn has disappeared, the black pawns in the middle stand on d6 and e6, and nothing blocks the rook's journey. With g7 empty, the white bishop again checks the king. The distant rook has time to come home because Black is busy answering someone else's attack.

When to stop collecting pieces

From b7 the rook returns to g7 with check. The black king goes to h8. This time White sends the rook down the g-file to g5, uncovering another bishop check. The rook now stands beside the black queen on h5. Black moves the king to h7, and White takes the queen. Compare the things that came off the board with the original diagram. The queens are gone, and Black has also lost two pawns and a bishop during the sequence.

The puzzle ends at that capture. In the actual game Lasker could still give Torre work to do, including an attack on both rook and bishop by his king. White eventually won. When practising the combination, give the final quiet capture as much attention as the checks. Continuing to shuttle the rook back and forth would be far less valuable than noticing the queen sitting just one square away.

Describe a windmill without using notation

For a second attempt, give yourself one job. After every white move say which piece is checking and which square it attacks. The answer alternates between a rook attacking along the g-file and a bishop cutting across to the corner. The exceptions at the beginning and end help explain the structure of the whole combination. White spends the first move arranging the pieces and the last collecting the queen.

Then imagine removing the bishop on f6 halfway through. As soon as the rook leaves g7, Black would have a free turn to use elsewhere. Imagine removing the rook instead and the bishop would be left giving a single diagonal check. The cycle needs both pieces, a king short of safe squares, and worthwhile targets within reach of the moving rook. Looking for those ingredients will serve you better than waiting to see Torre's exact position again.
